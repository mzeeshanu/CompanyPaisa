# Functional Requirements Document — CompanyPaisa

**Product:** CompanyPaisa.com — "Company + Paisa (money)"
**Author:** Zee
**Status:** Draft v0.2 — design agreed via clickable prototype, ready for Phase 1
**Last updated:** 2026-09-12 (executive lookup added)

> **Clickable prototype:** [`docs/prototype/home-prototype.html`](prototype/home-prototype.html) — open it in a browser.
> It is the visual reference for everything in §6 (UI & UX). Financial figures in it are **illustrative samples**, not real data.

---

## 1. Purpose & Vision

A web application that lets a user discover **publicly traded companies near their current location** and explore each company's financial story — revenue, net income, executive compensation, and up to ~10 years of history — to form a view on where the company is headed.

The defining constraint: **the app does not perform live third-party lookups at runtime.** All company, location, map and financial data is served from our own directory, curated ahead of time. When a user arrives, we take their location and assemble the result set entirely from local data.

---

## 2. Goals (v1)

- Given a user's location, return public companies with an office or HQ within a chosen radius.
- For each match, show the **specific nearby location** (HQ, campus, office, plant) and its distance — not just the corporate HQ.
- Show per company: profile basics, revenue & quarterly income, growth indicators, and executive compensation.
- Provide ~10 years of financial history for trend viewing.
- Present results in two switchable views (**Map** and **List**) with a distinctive "liquid glass" look in light and dark themes.
- Keep the data layer **swappable** (Excel/CSV now → SQL/NoSQL later via configuration only).

## 3. Non-Goals (v1)

- No live third-party lookups (Google Maps/Places, financial APIs, geocoders) at request time.
- No Google Maps — it requires Google's API. (See §6.5 for the self-hosted alternative.)
- No user accounts, watchlists, portfolios or trading.
- No automated predictions — we show history and simple indicators (YoY, CAGR, margin); modeling is later.
- No tracking/analytics/advertising cookies, and no third-party analytics. (The site counts visits itself, without visitor cookies or stored IP addresses; see the 2026-09-17 decision. The one other cookie, `cp_site`, is a security pass with no identifier — FR-18a.)
- No open public API: other apps need an API key (2026-09-22 decision).

---

## 4. Primary User & Key Scenario

**User:** Someone curious about the public companies physically around them — residents, job seekers, employees (e.g. "how is my employer doing vs. its neighbors?"), local investors.

**Core flow:**
1. User lands → page is blurred behind a small popup asking for **location or ZIP**.
2. User shares location (or types a ZIP) → the popup fades, the page un-blurs.
3. User sees nearby companies in their preferred view (**List** by default, or **Map**), filtered by radius.
4. User clicks a company (bubble or row) → the **company's own page** opens (`/company/AAPL`): key numbers, earnings history, executive pay, locations. Back returns to the same spot in the list.
5. First visit only: a small **cookie banner** asks whether to remember their view and theme.

## 5. Functional Requirements

### 5.1 Location & Discovery
- **FR-1** Accept location via browser geolocation **or** manual ZIP / city entry.
- **FR-1a** Never fire the browser permission prompt on page load — only after the user clicks "Use my location" (a denied prompt is sticky).
- **FR-1b** ZIP/city resolves via a **local US ZIP centroid table** (US Census Gazetteer, ~40k rows). No runtime geocoding; full street addresses are out of scope for v1.
- **FR-1c** If the user is far from any covered company, say so plainly and suggest a covered ZIP (don't show an empty page).
- **FR-2** Radius chips: **5 / 10 / 25 / 50 mi**, default **10 mi**.
- **FR-3** Return companies with **at least one location** inside the radius; each result shows its **nearest qualifying location** and straight-line (haversine) distance.
- **FR-4** Sort by **Revenue** (default), **Growth**, **Profit**, **Distance**.
- **FR-4a** Filters: **Sector** dropdown and **"Headquartered here only"** switch. Radius + filters apply to both views.

### 5.2 Company Directory
- **FR-5** Company record: display name, ticker, exchange, website, sector/industry, employees, market cap, description, fiscal year end, currency, logo URL.
- **FR-6** One or more locations per company: label/type (**HQ / campus / office / plant**), street, city, state, postal code, lat/long.
- **FR-7** Coordinates precomputed and stored — no runtime geocoding.
- **FR-7a** Location type drives the **HQ badge** and the "Headquartered here only" filter.

### 5.3 Financials
- **FR-8** Quarterly and annual revenue, net income (plus operating income, EPS later).
- **FR-9** Executive compensation per named executive per year: salary, bonus, stock awards, other, total (from DEF 14A proxy filings).
- **FR-10** Up to ~10 years of history.
- **FR-11** Time series shown as charts + tables.
- **FR-11a** Derived indicators computed **server-side in C#**: trailing-12-month (TTM) revenue & net income, YoY growth, 5-year CAGR, net margin, and a **trend status** (see §6.4).
- **FR-11b** Every financial figure should be traceable to its source filing (store `source_filing` URL).
- **FR-11c** Always label figures as **company-wide** when the nearby location is not the HQ.
- **FR-11d** Share price (2026-09-23): the latest price and a long-term chart, shown with **TradingView's free embedded widgets** (loaded in the visitor's browser from TradingView; we store no prices). Only for exchanges whose data TradingView allows in widgets; **nothing is shown** for the rest (LSE, PSX). The "hindsight" line (§6.7) needs prices of our own and is **deferred** until a price provider is chosen (open question 31).

### 5.4 Data Layer (Swappable Storage)
- **FR-12** All data access through one interface, `ICompanyRepository`.
- **FR-13** Live data: one SQLite file (`data/companypaisa.db`, one partition per market), loaded into memory at startup, validated, reloaded when an import replaces it. Excel workbooks remain a provider for the sample data.
- **FR-14** Later SQL (EF Core) / NoSQL implementations selected by config (`DataSource` connection string or file path) — no app-code changes.

### 5.5 Preferences & Consent
- **FR-15** Persist **view** (Map/List), **theme** (Auto/Light/Dark) and **map layer on/off**.
- **FR-16** Persist only after consent: first-party cookie `cp_prefs` (JSON, 1 year, `SameSite=Lax; Secure; path=/`).
- **FR-17** Consent banner appears after the location step (never stacked on top of the location popup): **"Remember my choices"** / **"Just this visit"**. Declining stores nothing and clears any earlier cookie. A **"Cookie settings"** link reopens it.
- **FR-18** Do not store the user's location without a separate, explicit opt-in (not in v1).
- **FR-18a** A strictly necessary security cookie, `cp_site` (HttpOnly, `SameSite=Strict`, `path=/api`, 12 hours, renewed while in use), set by every page: the website's pass to the API (2026-09-22). It holds only its expiry and the server's signature — no identifier — so it needs no consent; the privacy notice mentions it.
- **FR-15a** Also persist the lookup **mode** (Companies / Executives).

### 5.6 Executive Lookup (people)
- **FR-19** "Executives near me": list the **named executive officers of public companies headquartered within the radius** (or in the country / state searched) — the company's headquarters defines "near", never where a person lives (we don't store home locations). Companies with just an office in the area don't count (2026-09-24, requested by Zee: "only HQ companies' Executives should be shown"). API: `headquarteredOnly` on `/executives/near`, which the website always sends; without it the API keeps the older "any location in range" answer for other apps.
- **FR-20** A **person is one record across companies** (`person_id`). In real data `person_id` = the person's **SEC CIK** (reporting-owner id), so filings from different companies link to the same person.
- **FR-21** For each person show: current title and company, nearest location + distance, latest year's total pay, change vs prior year, **total pay over the last 10 years across all companies**, number of companies, pay-per-year sparkline (dots mark company changes).
- **FR-22** Sort by **Latest pay** (default), **10-year total**, **Pay growth**, **Distance**, **Name**. Filters: radius + sector (shared with Companies), **text search** on name/title, and **"Include people who moved away"** (former executives of nearby companies, flagged as former).
- **FR-23** Person profile: total earned (first–latest year), career as a list of roles (company, title, years, total pay), stacked pay-by-year chart **coloured by company**, and a year-by-year table (salary, bonus, stock, total). Company names link to the company page; executive names on the company page link to the person's page.
- **FR-24** Be explicit about scope: pay = **reported compensation** from DEF 14A summary compensation tables (stock at grant-date value, not realized); only roles as a **named executive officer of a public company** appear — private-company or non-NEO roles are not in SEC filings.
- **FR-25** API: `GET /api/v1/executives/near` (same location/radius/sector params as companies + `includeFormer`, `search`, `sort`, `years`) and `GET /api/v1/executives/{personId}`; available in the C# client. Behind the `Features:Executives` flag.

## 6. UI & UX Specification

### 6.1 Visual identity — "liquid glass"
- Frosted-glass panels and **glass-sphere bubbles** over a slowly drifting **aurora gradient** background.
- Rejected directions (don't revisit): dark "radar/sonar" look (sweep, crosshairs, compass letters), Google Maps background.
- **Type:** Sora (headings), Manrope (body), JetBrains Mono (numbers, tickers). Numbers use tabular figures.
- **Accent:** indigo `#3F48E0` (light) / `#8E95FF` (dark).
- **Status colors (separate from accent):** growing green `#0E9E67` / `#3DDC97`, flat amber `#D5870A` / `#F5B94E`, shrinking or loss-making red `#DB3E58` / `#FF6B81` (light / dark).
- Futuristic around the edges, **plain and readable inside the data** (panel, tables, charts).
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (stop aurora drift, pulses, animations).

### 6.2 Themes
- **Auto** (follows OS), **Light**, **Dark** — a small round icon button in the top bar's top-right corner cycles through them (the tooltip names the current one).
- Every color is a CSS token; dark is a designed palette (deep night-blue ground, dimmer aurora, brighter tints), not an inversion.

### 6.3 Page structure
- **Landing / location popup:** blurred page behind a glass card — headline "Who's making money around you?", **Use my location** button, a **place box** that takes a ZIP / postcode, a **city** ("Dallas", "Portland, OR" — the biggest place of that name when several states share it; centred there with the usual radius) or a **country, state or province** ("United States", "Texas", "Ontario", "UK", "Pakistan" — every company in it, see Region search below), clear error messages. Below the postcode box: "Looking for a particular company or person?" with the **search by name** box, so a visitor can go straight to a page without choosing a location.
- **Top bar (sticky, glass):**
  - Top line (slim, quiet): wordmark · 📍 "*Lehi, UT 84043* Change" (one button; phones show just "Lehi ▾") · Sample badge · on the right, the theme icon.
  - Search line: the **search by name** box (the largest control, full width on phones) · **Companies | Executives** switch · **Within 5/10/25/50 mi** dropdown · Sector dropdown · "HQ here only" switch (Companies only). On phones the line under the search box is Companies | Executives, a **Filters** button showing the range ("25 mi", with a "+1" badge when a sector is chosen) and the **HQ** switch pinned right (Companies only); tapping Filters opens radius and sector as a row underneath, so nothing scrolls sideways.
- **Company and executive pages:** every company and person has its own address — `/company/{TICKER}` and `/executive/{personId}` (e.g. `/executive/dylan-field-2073586`). Bubbles, rows and names are real links (long-press / middle-click / "open in new tab" work; a normal click moves inside the site without reloading). Page header: wordmark (back to the search) and theme button; under it "← Back to companies near Lehi" (or "Find public companies near you" when the page was opened from a shared link, which skips the location popup).
- **Search by name** (top bar on every screen, and the location popup; placeholder "Search a company, executive or place"): type 2+ letters of a company name, ticker or person's name — or a **place**: a country, state or province ("USA", "Utah", "Ontario") or a city ("Dallas") appears first under **Places** ("Every public company in the state" / "Public companies nearby") and opens that search (added 2026-09-23 after Zee typed "USA" and "UTAH" into the top box and got only company names; the API's `/search` returns `places`); up to 5 companies (name, ticker · HQ city, latest-12-month revenue) and 5 executives (name, latest title · company, latest pay) appear as you type. Arrow keys + Enter or a tap opens the page; Esc closes. Matching ignores case, accents and punctuation; every word typed must start a word of the name ("tim co" finds Tim Cook, "bank am" Bank of America); exact tickers first, then names that start with the text, then bigger companies / higher pay.
- **Region search (a whole country, state or province):** typing one in the place box, or opening `/near/texas`, `/near/united-kingdom`, `/near/ontario`, `/near/US-TX`, shows every company with a location there instead of a radius. Covers the countries in the data (US, Canada, UK, France, Netherlands, Italy, Spain, Pakistan, Australia, New Zealand), every US state and Canadian province, with the names and codes people type ("TX", "USA", "Britain", "Holland", "Québec"). "New York" and "Washington" mean the cities; a country after the name picks the state or province ("Washington, USA", "Washington US", "Ontario, Canada" — added 2026-09-23 at Zee's request, since plain "Washington" opens Washington, DC), as do "New York state" / "NY"; a city with its country also works ("Dallas, USA"), and the top search offers both for "Washington" (Washington, DC and Washington state); a two-letter "CA" is California, "NL" the Netherlands. Headline "3,833 public companies in the United States"; the radius control becomes an "All of Texas" chip; no distances and no Distance ranking; the bubble dropdown groups a whole US or Canadian search by state or province ("California · 677"), anything else by city, biggest first; company pages opened from it don't measure distances. Executives work the same way. API: `region` on `/companies/near` and `/executives/near` (a code or a name; `near=Texas` does the same), and `/geo/lookup` returns a `region` for such names. Each region with companies has its own server-rendered page ("Public companies in Texas") and is in the sitemap.
- **Search addresses:** the search on screen is always in the address bar, so it can be shared, bookmarked and reloaded: `/near/{place}` for companies, `/near/{place}/executives` for executives, plus only the settings that differ from the defaults — `radius`, `sector`, `hq=0` (HQ only switched off), `sort`, and for executives `q` (name/title filter) and `former=1`. Map / List and the theme stay personal preferences (cookie), not part of the address. `{place}` is the postcode without spaces (`84043`, `SW1A1AA`, `M5J2J2`), European / Australian / NZ / Pakistani codes with their country (`FR-75008`, `NL-1012AB`, `AU-2000`, `PK-74000`), or `me` for "my location" — the visitor's coordinates never go in the address. Opening a `/near/me` link uses the location only if the browser already allows it; otherwise the location popup asks. An unknown place opens the popup with "We couldn't find …". Changing filters updates the address without adding Back-button steps.

### 6.4 Bubbles (shared by both views)
- **Size = annual (TTM) revenue** using an **area (square-root) scale** with a **minimum size** so small companies stay clickable. Same scale everywhere so bubbles compare fairly.
- **Tint = trend status:**
  - `down` if TTM net income < 0, or YoY revenue < −1%
  - `up` if YoY revenue > +4% (and profitable)
  - `flat` otherwise
- Ticker centered on the bubble; name appears on hover / when zoomed in.
- **Hover:** tooltip with name, revenue, growth, distance. **Linked highlight:** hovering a bubble highlights its list row and vice versa.
- **Click:** opens the company's page; coming back, the page is scrolled to where it was and the last-opened bubble/row stays highlighted.

### 6.5 Map view — removed (2026-09-22)
- Removed at Zee's request ("I don't think I will ever go back to map mode… the box full of bubbles serves the purpose well"). The site is list-only: the wide bubble box (§6.6) shows how many companies there are and how big they are, the ranked list gives the detail. The map's code, styles and settings (`Ui:DefaultView`, `Ui:Map`, `Features:MapView` / `ListView`) are gone; the earlier design is in git history (before 2026-09-22) and in `docs/prototype/home-prototype.html`.

### 6.6 List view
1. **Summary line:** "**15 public companies** within 10 miles of Lehi" · combined TTM revenue · # growing · # headquartered here.
2. **Bubbles:** one wide glass box with every company's bubble spread left to right (no overlap, biggest near the middle), so it takes the page's width rather than its height; on narrower screens every bubble shrinks alike. The bubbles come from a small list of every company in the area (ticker, name, revenue, trend, growth, city, state, distance, HQ), fetched once per search — not with every page of the ranked list — and are laid out by circle packing that grows into a wide oval (d3's front-chain packing with sideways distance counting for less): no overlaps, about 50 ms for 3,800 companies. **Big areas keep every company:** the box has a height limit (about 45% of its width on desktop, 340–560px; about square on phones) and when there are too many companies every bubble shrinks by the same factor to fit, the smallest becoming dots (still hoverable and tappable), so the box still shows how many companies there are and how they compare (e.g. 336 around Sunnyvale). An **Enlarge / Fit to box** button (only shown when bubbles were shrunk) lets the box grow to full-size bubbles instead. **Pop-in:** when a search's results arrive, the box is already at its final size and each bubble grows from nothing with a small overshoot, **biggest first** (the last one starts after 0.2–0.65 s depending on how many there are; each takes 0.52 s). Not replayed when coming back from a company page, switching city, enlarging or resizing; off for visitors whose device asks for reduced motion. A **city dropdown** ("All cities · 58", then each city nearest first: "Draper · 5 · 5.9 mi") narrows the box to one city. Legend beside it: size = annual revenue, growing / flat / shrinking tints.
3. **Ranked list:** loaded **100 rows at a time** ("Showing 100 of 3,833 · Show 100 more"); the ranking, the reversed order and the search are done by the API, so a whole country never arrives as one answer. Rank-by chips (Revenue / Growth / Profit / Distance) and a **"Search name or ticker…" box** that narrows the list only — the bubbles and summary line keep the whole area; ranks renumber, "Showing 6 of 128 · Clear" under the rows, "No company here matches …" when nothing does; kept in the address as `q`, cleared when a new area is picked. Columns: rank · mini bubble · name + HQ badge + ticker/sector · nearest location + city + distance · TTM revenue · growth pill · revenue sparkline since 2017 · TTM net income. Fewer columns on tablet/mobile. **Column headers are tappable** (both Companies and Executives lists): tap a header to sort by it; tap the active header again to reverse the order (▼/▲ shows direction; rows with no value stay at the bottom). On phones the list always fits the screen width (no sideways scrolling); the Rank-by chips swipe sideways.
4. Empty state: "Nothing matches. Widen the radius or clear the filters."

### 6.7 Company page (`/company/{TICKER}`)
- Header card: ticker chip, exchange, sector, industry; company name; trend dot · headquarters city · **nearest location's distance from the visitor's search point** (when they have one); employees and website when known; description; **KPI tiles** in one row (two per row on phones): revenue (latest quarter, or latest fiscal year for yearly-only filers), net income, revenue growth (TTM vs prior TTM), 5-year CAGR; note "Company-wide figures, not just one location".
- **Earnings** card: Quarterly (last 12 quarters) / Annual (~10 FY) toggle; bars = revenue, line = net income (dots green/red by sign), latest period emphasized; table of the last 4 quarters or last 10 years with YoY; periods link to the filing.
- **Layout:** under the header card and the full-width New leadership card, two columns on wide screens — left: **Earnings**, then **Executive pay**, **CEO vs the typical employee** and **Salaries by job title** (the numbers); right: **Share price** (2026-09-23, moved here by Zee: "top of the right side above the at a glance"), **At a glance**, **Pay vs similar companies**, **Similar companies nearby**, **Locations** (what they mean). One column on narrow screens in that same order (2026-09-18, requested by Zee: "left side first Earnings, below that executive earnings, right side other info").
- **Share price** card (top of the right column, above At a glance; on phones the **first card**, above Earnings — requested by Zee, "on the phone move it to the top") (2026-09-23): on the heading row, the ticker and full exchange name ("AAPL · Nasdaq Stock Market", "JPM · New York Stock Exchange"; requested by Zee 2026-09-24 — the widget ignores a label we pass, so it is our own text); below it one TradingView **Symbol Overview** widget, 300px tall: company name, price, change over the chosen range, and a chart with 1-year, 5-year and all-time ranges (made smaller 2026-09-24 at Zee's request, "can we make the stock widget size smaller?": the separate Symbol Info box with market cap, P/E, dividend yield and next earnings date was dropped, taking the card from about 630px to 430px). Theme follows the site's light/dark theme, transparent background; loaded only when the card scrolls into view, so it doesn't slow the page. TradingView's "by TradingView" link stays under the widgets (the condition of the free widgets). Prices are delayed, not live (TradingView marks them "D").
  - **Which companies:** only listings on exchanges that work in the widgets, kept as a config list mapping our exchange names to TradingView's prefixes (`PriceWidget:Exchanges`; the server sends the symbol, e.g. "NASDAQ:AAPL", "MIL:ENI", or none). Tested 2026-09-23: **works** — Nasdaq, NYSE, NYSE American and NYSE Arca (TradingView's `AMEX`), TSX, Euronext Paris, Euronext Amsterdam, Borsa Italiana (MIL), BME Madrid; **blocked** ("This symbol is only available on TradingView") — **LSE, PSX**. Companies on a blocked or untested exchange get **no card at all** (no placeholder, no message), and so do tickers TradingView spells differently (preferred shares, units, warrants such as "SNUS-PH"); share classes are converted ("BRK-B" → "BRK.B"). The exchange prefix is required: a bare ticker can pick another listing ("RY" gives Toronto in CAD, not the NYSE listing in USD).
  - **Privacy:** the widgets set no cookies, so no consent is needed; TradingView receives the page address, widget type, symbol and the visitor's IP (kept briefly, per their FAQ). The privacy notice gets a line saying so.
  - **Hindsight line — deferred** until we have prices of our own (open question 31); agreed design kept here for then. Shown only when **both** hold:
    1. the latest price is **above the month-end price 10 years ago** (or at listing, for newer companies) — a stock that fell and only partly recovered never gets it, and neither does one that went down and never came back up;
    2. the latest price is **at least 2×** the lowest month-end close in the period.

    It starts from that **lowest month-end close** and works back from a million: amount = 1,000,000 ÷ (latest ÷ lowest), rounded to 3 significant figures. "📈 **Hindsight:** $34,800 invested at Apple's lowest month-end price in the last 10 years (Jan 2019) would be worth **$1 million** today." Also hidden when the latest price is more than double or less than half the last month-end close (most likely a split the monthly history hasn't picked up yet). Fine print: "Past performance isn't a guide to future returns. Prices adjusted for splits and dividends; month-end closes, so the low is the lowest month-end price, not the lowest trade."
- **Executive pay** card (beside Earnings on wide screens, below it on narrow ones): per named executive — title, name (link to their page), latest total pay + change vs prior year, stacked bar (salary / bonus / stock / other, scaled to the top earner), 5-year pay sparkline.
- **CEO vs the typical employee** card (left column, after Executive pay; US companies whose proxy discloses a pay ratio): the ratio in large type ("647×"), "In 2025 the CEO was paid $74.3M, 647 times the $115K the company's median employee earned. The CEO made the median employee's yearly pay in about 1 day"; two bars (median employee vs CEO, to scale); earlier years' ratios; a link to the proxy statement; fine print that the median employee is the middle of all employees worldwide, part-time included (2026-09-22).
- **Salaries by job title** card (left column, after the pay ratio; US companies): a switch between the sources when there are two — **Job ads** (the pay ranges in the company's own US job ads, shown first: they cover every kind of job it hires for) and **Visa filings** (H-1B) — each with its title count; a search box when there are more than 8 titles; per job title — name, number of filings and the government occupation, the median yearly salary, and a bar spanning the middle half of the offers (25th–75th percentile) on a scale shared by the rows shown; tapping a title lists its work places (city, median, range, filings), nearest the visitor's search point first when they have one. First 12 titles, then "Show all"; an open job ad links out ("See a current ad ↗"). Fine print per source: for job ads, that pay-transparency laws in states such as California, Colorado, New York and Washington require a range, and that it is advertised base pay before bonuses and stock; for visa filings, that they cover roles hired from abroad for (mostly professional), state the committed salary, and that a job shows only with at least three filings (2026-09-22).
- **At a glance** card ("{Company} at a glance"): short facts worked out from the company's figures and the rest of the data set; each appears only when the data supports it. (1) CEO pay change vs revenue change for the same fiscal year ("CEO X's pay rose 23% to $2.8M in 2025, while revenue for fiscal 2025 rose 14%"). (2) Rank by latest-12-month revenue in its sector, shown when top 10 or top 10% ("#5 by revenue among the 77 Semiconductors companies we track"). (3) Rank among public companies headquartered in the same city, shown when top 5. (4) Revenue streak: 3+ quarters (or years, for yearly filers) in a row of growth or decline against a year before. (5) Best revenue year ("2025 was its best year yet"). (6) Profitable years out of the last ten. (7) The CEO earns a typical worker's yearly pay every N hours (same benchmark as the quick fact). (8) Net margin vs the sector median ("Keeps 28¢ of every $1 of revenue as profit; the median Technology hardware company keeps 1¢"). (9) Revenue per employee when the employee count is known, otherwise revenue per second. Ranks, sector medians and "similar" skip companies without a real sector (European filers, sector "Other").
- **New leadership** card (below At a glance, when the company announced officer appointments in the last 18 months): per person — name (links to their page when they have reported pay anywhere), title, "Announced 16 Apr 2026 · starts 5 Aug 2026", the **announced package** total, a stacked bar and list of its parts (base salary, bonus stated in dollars, sign-on / make-whole cash, stock awards, performance stock, options, relocation / other cash; same-label grants added up, "(2 grants)"), and "Read the announcement (8-K) ↗". First 3, then "Show all". Fine print: what the company said it would pay when it announced the appointment, not pay received; stock at the stated value; percentage bonus targets not counted; read automatically, check the filing. The newest appointment of the last year is also the first **At a glance** fact: "New President and Chief Executive Officer Terrence O. Moorehead joined on 5 Aug 2026 with an announced package of $8.8M."
- **Pay vs similar companies** card (left column, after At a glance; hidden when too few peers report pay): headline "Pays its CEO more than 64% / less than 72% of similar companies" (about the middle between 45% and 55%); two tiles — CEO pay and the other named executives' median pay, each with the peers' median and the % difference, and the other executives' standing ("paid less than 85% of similar"); then "The 8 companies closest in size, by CEO pay": those companies and this one (highlighted) ranked highest pay first, with bars and links to each company. **Similar** = same sector (not the European "Other"), same kind of filing (UK companies only against UK companies: their reports give executive directors' "single total figure", not a CEO line; Pakistani companies only against Pakistani companies), latest reported pay no more than a year older than this company's, revenue between a third and three times this company's — or, when that gives fewer than 20, the 20 closest in size. Fine print names how many peers and the revenue range they span. Pay in this company's pay currency (others converted at the approximate rates). UK: the top-paid executive director stands in for the CEO.
- **Similar companies nearby** card: up to 5 companies closest to its headquarters in the same sector within 100 miles (distance in whole miles, bigger companies first on ties; "same area" under a mile); when fewer than 2, "Companies nearby" of any sector within 25 miles. Each row: trend dot, name, ticker · city · distance, latest-12-month revenue, growth pill; links to that company's page.

### 6.7a Salaries page (`/company/{TICKER}/salaries`)

### 6.8 Executives view & person page
- **Summary line:** "**294 executives** at 42 public companies headquartered within 25 miles of Lehi" · combined pay (latest year) · median pay · scope note ("named executive officers only").

## 9. Data Sourcing

- **Coverage (2026-09-13):** the top 20 US metros plus the Wasatch Front, discovered automatically from SEC filings by business address (see decisions log). - **Financials & exec pay:** an **offline C# import tool** pulls from SEC EDGAR (10-K, 10-Q, DEF 14A / XBRL) into the workbook. Runs ahead of time, never at request time.
- **Locations:** entered by hand (address + lat/long), checked against filings/websites.
- **Coverage (2026-09-14):** every listed US company, Canadian companies that file with the SEC, UK Main Market companies, and listed companies in France, the Netherlands, Italy and Spain (financials only), and Australian / New Zealand companies that file with the SEC — all from official filings (see decisions log). - **Workers' pay (2026-09-22):** the CEO pay ratio from US proxy statements (read during the SEC import); salaries by job title from the US Department of Labor's H-1B wage filings (`--salaries`, latest two fiscal years) and from the pay ranges in companies' own job ads (`--postings`, their boards on Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters and Workday) — see decisions log.
- **Share prices (2026-09-23):** not stored — TradingView's widgets fetch them in the visitor's browser (§6.7). The exact US exchange (NYSE / NYSE American / NYSE Arca / Cboe) comes from Nasdaq Trader's free symbol directory, read by the SEC import and by `--enrich exchanges`. For the deferred hindsight line: a paid end-of-day provider (open question 31), fetched offline; the monthly refresh would re-download each company's 10 years of adjusted month-end closes (one call per company).
- **Refresh cadence:** monthly, by a scheduled task on Zee's PC (`tools/refresh-data.ps1`); the UI shows "Data as of …".
